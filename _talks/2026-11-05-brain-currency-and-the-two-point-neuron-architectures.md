Co-authored with Tomas Marvan (Czech Academy of Sciences). Presented in the session *(Neuro)philosophy of (neuro)science*.

We challenge the dominant Predictive Processing paradigm, arguing that its reliance on single-purpose prediction errors yields an ontologically impoverished, purely reactive model of the mind. Grounding our alternative in the biophysics of layer 5 pyramidal "two-point" neurons—where top-down contextual signals amplify rather than suppress bottom-up input—we propose that the brain's fundamental currency is *contextually amplified representations*, not error signals. This supports a multi-purpose computational architecture capable of decoupled, offline cognition and genuine representation of the external world.
