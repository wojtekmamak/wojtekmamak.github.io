Can the hippocampal–entorhinal "navigation" machinery provide an architectural template for propositional thought? Grid-like and distance codes have now been found not only for physical space, but also for abstract semantic and social spaces. Yet there is a substantial gap between showing that entorhinal and frontal cortices encode relational structure in a map-like way and explaining how such codes underwrite propositional content and inference.

In this talk I argue that Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA), interpreted within a Correspondence Network Framework, supplies the missing interpretive layer. The entorhinal–frontal patterns revealed by RSA (e.g. Viganò et al., 2021) are best understood as *navigable correspondence networks*: the grid-like code supplies a compressed, low-dimensional metric lattice, while distance-sensitive codes in medial prefrontal regions implement evaluative and control operations over it. Propositional operations—comparative judgments, simple conditionals, counterfactual traversals—can then be modelled as constrained trajectories through, and evaluations of, this network. Genuine generativity, on this account, is not recombination of points on a fixed map but topological change in the correspondence network itself. Finally, I connect the picture to an information-based account of content: whether a pattern has propositional content depends on its exploitability by downstream consumers, which lets us tell merely correlationally structured patterns from those that actually figure in propositional thinking.
