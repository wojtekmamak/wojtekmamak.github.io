What does it take for a process to genuinely generate information, rather than merely recombine or interpolate what is already there? Despite the ubiquity of "generative" talk in the age of generative AI, the notion has remained virtually untouched in the philosophical literature. In this talk I separate faux-generativity from genuine generativity, argue that the interpolation/extrapolation distinction from machine learning is not sufficient to tell them apart, and offer a cluster definition of genuine information-generation based on content-preserving inferences over information stored in compressed internal representations—the "real-generation account".

*Based on the paper [On Generating Information](/publication/generativity) (under review).*
