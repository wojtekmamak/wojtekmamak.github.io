***[Miesięcznik Znak]***

**Outline:**

This essay defends the philosophy of science against its most persistent public and academic critique: that it is fundamentally useless. Beginning with a Polish political anecdote—where a millionaire escaped tax evasion charges by citing his "philosophical education and detachment from material reality"—the article unpacks the pervasive stereotype of the philosopher as a disconnected dreamer. 

The narrative then takes this skepticism head-on, moving from internet trolls asking if philosophy will "invent a cure for cancer" to the highest echelons of physics. By examining a 2015 conference at Ludwig Maximilian University, where theoretical physicists hit a methodological wall regarding string theory and the multiverse, I show how scientists were forced to summon philosophers of science to help define their own foundational concepts. Addressing Feynman's famous quip that "scientists need philosophers as much as birds need ornithologists," the piece ultimately adopts philosopher Tim Maudlin's brilliant retort: ornithology would actually be incredibly useful to birds—it would stop them from smashing into windows.
