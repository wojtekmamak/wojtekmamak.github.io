Co-authored with Tomas Marvan, this draft paper challenges the dominant Predictive Processing (PP) paradigm, arguing that its reliance on single-purpose prediction errors results in an ontologically impoverished, purely reactive model of the mind. To resolve this philosophical deadlock, we ground our alternative in the biophysics of layer 5 pyramidal "two-point" neurons, where top-down contextual signals amplify—rather than suppress—bottom-up sensory inputs. By shifting the brain's fundamental currency from fragile error signals to "contextually amplified representations," we defend a multi-purpose computational architecture capable of supporting true decoupled, offline cognition and genuine representation of the external world.
